What to look for

A short guide to the current Admin Center build.

Back to workspace
Current data: Bot, learning and model examples are synthetic unless a feed says otherwise. The hub is not configured; lead and coordinator panels use read-only local records; bridge status uses a read-only SSH observation. Model changes remain disabled.

Try these

  1. FFXI playtest focus. The read-only summary identifies the documented party-greeting candidate, displays FFXI data-feed status and fetch times, and shows a timestamped bridge observation. Feed status is not proof of game presence or delivery. Choose “View FFXI bot roster” to filter the loaded roster; “Clear roster filters” restores all games. Roster entries do not establish presence. If a selected bot is hidden by a roster filter or missing from the loaded snapshot, the roster count says so; “Clear bot selection” clears the inspector. Choose “View FFXI learning needs” to filter the loaded learning records; “Clear learning filters” restores all games. Choose “View FFXI issues” for FFXI-scoped loaded issues, including the historical bridge report; its “View current bridge observation” button navigates to the read-only status card; “Clear issue filters” restores all scopes. Neither shortcut establishes complete coverage. “Open FFXI evidence search” selects FFXI and clears any old correlation without calling the hub; enter a correlation and submit to search. After the FFXI lead confirms readiness, follow their test instructions and look for a visible reply. This Admin Center has no connected behavior toggle; the actual gate state, active source and player-visible delivery are unverified. The bridge fix was approved, but its deployment receipt is still pending here.
  2. See what changed. Open the workspace and read “What's new” and the hub status banner. The source selector defaults to synthetic examples; selecting “Try configured read-only hub” currently shows fallback until access is configured. Once configured, refreshes may share a 15-second snapshot; each feed keeps its fetch time. A duplicate-key roster or duplicate-ID learning feed is unavailable and its feed explains the synthetic fallback. While loading or if refresh fails, the banner identifies the previous request source and snapshot time; retry with Refresh.
  3. Inspect a bot. Check the per-game loaded-roster counts, filter by game or name, and choose “Inspect” on an entry. “Clear roster filters” restores the loaded list. Check its displayed character, level, body-kind and reported personality summary, then its linked example evidence and issues. Use “View [game] game learning” to see loaded game-wide learning records; these are not attributed to the selected bot. Roster metadata is not proof of presence or behavior. One bot has synthetic proposed-goal and unverified-memory examples; live goals and memory remain unavailable. Open “Model routing (read-only)” for one bot, or use “Show visible routing details” and “Hide visible routing details” for the filtered list; these only change display, and purpose and model controls cannot be changed. The reply source panel shows synthetic fallback and delivery evidence separately. “Clear bot selection” clears the inspector and linked views.
  4. Explore learning needs. Filter by game or status, search by topic or reason, check the per-game loaded-record coverage, and inspect a need for its phase, evidence and policy state. Open “Game learning planning checklist” to show or hide planned FFXI and SWTOR topics; the game filter applies. These are not verified gaps, and evidence, datasets and policies remain unknown. Open “Dataset and policy examples” to inspect synthetic validation, adoption and outcome states separately; the game filter applies. The count warns when a selected need is hidden by the current filter or missing from the loaded snapshot; its inspector remains selected. Use “Clear learning selection” to clear it without changing filters. Related issues appear when explicitly linked; missing evidence is shown as unknown or not included. Toggle “Show known attention statuses only” to narrow the list to the named statuses; use “Clear learning filters” to restore it.
  5. Inspect service health. With hub data selected, “Check hub service health” requests a read-only snapshot. It is unavailable until the hub is configured; service state does not establish bot presence.
  6. Check game settings. The game settings card shows a stored snapshot where available. Its time and source matter; it does not establish whether the server is online.
  7. Watch the bridge. The FFXI chat bridge card shows the unit, fault marker and its known classification from a read-only observation, refreshing while the workspace is visible. An absent marker does not prove a reply was delivered.
  8. Review issues. Filter by scope or status, or search. Toggle “Show issues linked to the selected bot” after selecting a bot to show explicit links only. Scope counts cover only loaded records, not all incidents; “Clear issue filters” restores the loaded list. The FFXI bridge fault card describes historical project evidence; its current status is unknown. Coordinator decision cards link to the read-only “Needs Blizz” summary.
  9. Check lead activity. Compare the lead stall and quota summary with the timeline and nudge history. Shared quota estimates are not individual lead quota, and activity after a nudge is only a heuristic.
  10. Inspect safety examples. The gates and stop-controls card shows synthetic network and selected-bot examples. Actual gate and kill-switch states are unknown, and stop buttons are disabled.
  11. Review the spend example. The spend card is synthetic and does not read the live ledger. Unknown budget and per-bot attribution are not zero.
  12. Follow evidence. In a bot’s example evidence, choose “View example episode in ledger” to filter the synthetic ledger, or enter a correlation ID manually. This navigation does not call the hub. After the selected hub source loads successfully, the same search can request a bounded read-only hub trace without raw message bodies. Changing the game or correlation input clears the old hub trace. A request, its result and its verification are separate; a dispatched request alone does not prove completion.
